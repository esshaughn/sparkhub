How it works

Ideas in. Plans out.

Spark Hub is for two kinds of people: the neighbors on your block, and the friends you already make plans with.

Neighborhood groups

A group belongs to a place.

Groups belong to a place

Each group has a middle and an area around it, so you know it's the one for your block.

Join by link or QR code

Groups aren't listed or searchable. Someone shares a link, or you scan a code at an event.

Who can join

Before joining, you say how you’re connected. Leads decide who the group is for.

I live hereI live nearbyJust visiting

Keeping it safe

Every group has a lead and a co-lead. Anyone can report a post, and we review reports within 72 hours.

Questions

When does the beta end?+

We don’t have a date yet. Leads will hear from us well before anything changes.

Will Friends and Seed groups stay free?+

Yes. Making plans with your friends and starting a group are free.

Who pays for a neighborhood group?+

The group, not its people. Joining a group, RSVPing and signing up are always free.

What happens to our events if we don’t pay?+

Nothing gets deleted. Your events, photos and people stay put.

Do you sell our information or show ads?+

No. There are no ads, and we don’t sell anything about you.

For neighborhoods

Bring Spark Hub to your neighborhood.

Starting a group is a big deal. You're making a place where your neighbors can show up for each other.

Start a group

Four steps

Draft it. Build the team. Open it.

  1. 1

    Draft it

    Name the group, set its place, and say who it’s for.

  2. 2

    Build the team

    Ask a co-lead (required), then admins and ambassadors.

  3. 3

    Send it for review

    We read every request and get back to you.

  4. 4

    Open it

    Share the link and the QR code. Plan your first event.

The team

No one runs it alone.

Required

A lead

You. You start the group and look after it.

Required

A co-lead

Someone who shares the work and can step in when you’re away.

Recommended

A team of admins

A few neighbors who help plan events, welcome people and keep it friendly.

Associations first

Got a neighborhood association? Start there.

If your neighborhood already has one, we'll ask them first. A group that works with the people already doing the work lasts longer.

What leads agree to

  • Keep it open to the neighborhood, not just your friends.
  • Follow the community rules, and help others follow them.
  • Look at reports in your group.
  • Hand the group to someone else if you step away.

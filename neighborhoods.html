For neighborhoods

Bring Spark Hub to your neighborhood.

Starting a group gives you a real say in it, and real responsibility for it: decisions, members, reports. Nobody should carry that alone, so a group starts the way an event does here. You draft it, find your team, and open it together.

Start a requestStart a draft. You'll need an account, and a team before it opens.
How it goes

Draft it. Build the team. Open it.

1

Draft your group

Where it is, what it's for, and how it'll run: who can join, and the settings you'll start with.

2

Find your team

Ask a co-lead and a few admins to join you, and people willing to spread the word. They say yes in the app.

3

We talk

We get in touch, answer questions, and check in with the neighborhood association if there is one.

4

Open it

You get the link and QR code to share, and help planning how to get your neighbors on board.

Who runs it

At least two people, ideally a team.

A lead

The person responsible for the group, including answering reports from members.

A co-lead

Shares the responsibility, so the group never depends on one person. Required to start.

A team of admins

Strongly recommended: one to three people who help run things and make decisions together.

Associations first

If your neighborhood has an association, start there.

Where there's a neighborhood association or HOA, we'd rather the group belong to it than to whoever asks first. A resident can start a group and hand it to the association later.

What leads agree to

Looking after the group.

  • Answer reports and keep the group kind and safe
  • Keep it open to the neighborhood, not a private club
  • No selling or political campaigning in the group
  • Follow the community rules

Groups are free during the beta. See what plans will look like after it.

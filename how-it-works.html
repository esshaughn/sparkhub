How it works

Groups for neighborhoods. Lists for friends.

Spark Hub has two sides that share one calendar: groups, for a real place and the people around it, and lists, for the friends you plan with.

Groups

A group belongs to a place.

A group is for a neighborhood, a school, a building or a park: somewhere people can actually meet. Each one has a lead and a co-lead who are responsible for it, and usually a small team of admins.

Joining

Groups aren't listed or searchable. You join with the group's link or QR code, which the group shares itself: on flyers, in a newsletter, or neighbor to neighbor. Before you join you see where the group is, so you know it's the right one.

Who can join

The group's leads decide: neighbors only, neighbors and nearby, or everyone with the link. They can change it any time, and remove anyone who shouldn't be there.

Ideas

Anyone in the group can float an idea. Others say they're interested or offer to help, and someone takes the lead.

Plans

A plan has a date and a place, or a vote on them. People RSVP, bring guests, and get reminders on their phone.

Pitching in

Leads list the jobs and shifts it takes. People sign up, swap out, or join a waitlist when it's full.

Keeping it safe

Anyone can report a comment, an event, a photo or a person. The group's leads see reports for their group, and can remove or block people.

Friends

Plans with the people you already know.

You don't need a group to plan with friends. Add friends with a link, keep lists of the people you usually invite, and make private events just for them.

  • Private events are seen only by the people you invite, up to 20
  • Lists stay private to you: nobody sees a list or that they're on it
  • Your friends' plans and your groups' plans land on the same calendar
  • Inside a group, you can also make a small private gathering with a list of its members
Guests

No account needed to say yes.

Share an event's link with anyone. They can see it and RSVP with their first name, and make an account later if they want one.

On your phone

Add it to your Home Screen.

Spark Hub runs in your browser. Add it to your Home Screen and it opens like an app, with notifications for the things you're part of.

Want a group for your neighborhood? Groups start by request, with a lead and a co-lead, so every one has people looking after it.
